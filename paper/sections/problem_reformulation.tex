\section{Problem Reformulation}
\label{Section: Problem Reformulation}

Deep hashing aims to learn network parameters \(\bx \in \RR^{\blue{q}}\) that generate binary codes preserving semantic similarity in the Hamming space. A direct formulation is
\begin{align}
\label{Eq:direct_deep_hashing_problem}
\blue{\min_{\bx \in \RR^{q}}\ \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\ell_{s,i}\!\big(\operatorname{sgn}(\mathcal{D}(\bx))\big),}
\end{align}
where \(n\) is the number of training samples, \blue{\(\bD_i:\RR^q\to\RR^d\) is the continuous network output mapping for sample \(i\), and \(\ell_{s,i}:\RR^{n\times d}\to\RR\) is a similarity-preserving loss} (e.g., pairwise cross-entropy \cite{zhuDeepHashingNetwork2016}). This objective is hard to optimize by backpropagation because \(\operatorname{sgn}(\cdot)\) is nondifferentiable and has zero gradient almost everywhere. 

\blue{To address this issue, penalties with W-shaped graphs, vanishing exactly at
$\pm1$, are added to drive the continuous outputs toward $\{-1,1\}^d$. Such penalties
are standard in deep hashing~\cite{liuDeepSupervisedHashing,wang2016DTSH,
zhuDeepHashingNetwork2016,yuanCentralSimilarityQuantization2020,he2025mambahash,
yin2026crh}. Their formulas differ, but our analysis depends only on their common
shape, which we formalize in \Cref{Section: W-regularizer}.}
\subsection{W-type Regularizer: Definition and Properties}
\label{Section: W-regularizer}
\begingroup
\color{blue}
We now define the W-type class and list representative examples, summarized in \Cref{tab:wtype} in this section.
\begin{definition}[W-type regularizer]\label{def:wtype}
Let \(\phi:\mathbb{R}\to[0,+\infty)\). For \(\bz\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\) and \(\bZ\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}\), define \(h(\bz):=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\phi(z_j)\) and \(h(\bZ):=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\phi(z_{ij})\). We call \(h\) a \emph{W-type regularizer} if \(\phi\) satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(W\arabic*)}]
  \item\label{W1} \(\phi\) is even and continuous, and \(\phi(t)=0\) if and only if \(t\in\{-1,1\}\);
  \item\label{W2} \(\phi\) is convex on \([1,+\infty)\);
  \item\label{W3} \(\phi\) decreases at a nonvanishing rate on \([0,1)\): for every \(b\in(0,1)\) there exists \(c_b>0\) such that \(\phi(s)-\phi(t)\ge c_b(t-s)\) for all \(0\le s<t\le b\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

Each condition is used once: \ref{W1} makes the zero set of \(h\) exactly \(\{-1,1\}^{n\times d}\); \ref{W2} yields the envelope formula of Lemma~\ref{lem:wtype-conj}, so that the dual reformulation leaves \(h\) unchanged on \(|t|\ge1\); and \ref{W3} excludes stationary points in \((-1,1)\) (Lemma~\ref{lem:no-inner-stationary}), which gives the stationarity-to-contact implication of Proposition~\ref{prop:transfer}.

Every row of Table~\ref{tab:wtype} is nondifferentiable at \(0\), where \(\partial\phi(0)=\{\pm c\}\) for some \(c>0\), and the row \(p=1\) is also nondifferentiable at the binary codes \(\pm1\); hence none of these penalties is \(L\)-smooth or weakly convex, and standard complexity guarantees for gradient methods do not apply. Tail growth determines \(\operatorname{dom}\phi^*\): the complexity analysis in Section~\ref{Section: Convergence analysis} covers the rows with \(\operatorname{dom}\phi^*=[-\lambda,\lambda]\), while Proposition~\ref{prop:transfer} also covers \(p=2\).
\begin{remark}[Role of \ref{W2} and \ref{W3}]\label{rem:wtype-scope}
Neither condition can be dropped from our analysis. Without \ref{W3}, take
\(\phi(t)=|t^2-1|\), which satisfies \ref{W1}--\ref{W2} but has \(\partial\phi(0)=\{0\}\):
with \(n=d=1\), no \(\bx\)-block and \(F(b)=\tfrac12 b^2\), the point \(b=0\) is stationary
for Problem~\eqref{Prop: block-wise regularized problem}, since \(J(b)=1-\tfrac12 b^2\)
near \(b=0\), yet \(|b|<1\); hence the conclusion of Proposition~\ref{prop:transfer} fails.
Without \ref{W2}, take \(\phi(t)=\lambda\log\bigl(1+\bigl||t|-1\bigr|\bigr)\), which
satisfies \ref{W1} and \ref{W3} but grows sublinearly; then
\(\operatorname{dom}\phi^*=\{0\}\) and \(h^{**}\equiv0\), so the dual reformulation removes
the regularizer. Neither condition is needed to define the W shape itself; both are needed for our analysis.
\end{remark}

\subsection{W-type Regularized Deep Hashing Model}
\label{Sec:W-type Regularized Deep Hashing Model}
Combining the W-type regularization \(h\) in \Cref{tab:wtype},  the model is now trained to produce the \(i\)-th continuous network output \(\bD_i(\bx)\), and binary codes are obtained post-training via \(\operatorname{sgn}(\bD_i(\bx))\blue{\in\{-1,1\}^d}\). \blue{Consequently, we formulate the following core optimization problem for W-type regularized deep hashing methods, which serves as a continuous surrogate of Problem~\eqref{Eq:direct_deep_hashing_problem}:}
\begin{align}
\label{Eq: the primal deep hashing problem}
\min_{\bx \in \RR^{\blue{q}}} \quad \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n f_i(\bx) + \sum_{i=1}^n h(\bD_i(\bx)),
\tag{P0}
\end{align}\blue{where
\(f_i(\bx):=\ell_{s,i}\!\left(\mathcal{D}(\bx)\right)+\frac{\mu}{2}\|\bx\|^2\) with \(\mu>0\) ensures coercivity.} We then employ variable splitting by introducing \blue{continuous auxiliary variables \(\bb_i\in\RR^d\) and \(\bB=[\bb_1^\top;\ldots;\bb_n^\top]\in\RR^{n\times d}\), subject to \(\bb_i=\bD_i(\bx)\) for all \(i=1,\dots,n\),} which yields the following equivalent nonlinearly constrained problem:
\begin{align}
    \blue{\min_{\bx \in \RR^{q},\ \bB \in \RR^{n\times d}}\quad \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} f_i(\bx)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}h(\bb_i)\quad \text{s.t.}\quad \bb_i=\bD_i(\bx),\ \forall i=1,\dots,n.} \notag
\end{align}
We then \blue{relax these constraints by} the quadratic penalty method \cite[Sec.~17.1]{nocedalWright2006} to obtain the following two-block finite-sum optimization problem:
\begin{align}
    \label{Prop: block-wise regularized problem}
    \blue{\min_{\bx \in \RR^{q},\,\bB \in \RR^{n\times d}}\ J(\bx,\bB):=F(\bx,\bB)+h(\bB),} \tag{P}
\end{align}
\blue{Here, \(\gamma>0\) is the penalty parameter, and we use the separable extension \(h(\bB):=\sum_{i=1}^{n}h(\bb_i)\) for matrices. We define}
\begin{align}
    \blue{F(\bx,\bB):= \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}F_i(\bx,\bb_i),\qquad F_i(\bx,\bb_i):= f_i(\bx)+\frac{\gamma}{2}\|\bD_i(\bx)-\bb_i\|^2.} \notag
\end{align}
\blue{Averaging over \(i\) gives the decomposition \(F=f+P\), where}
\begin{align}
    \blue{f(\bx):= \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_i(\bx),\qquad P(\bx,\bB):= \frac{\gamma}{2n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\bD_i(\bx)-\bb_i\|^2.} \notag
\end{align}

This reformulation decomposes the original problem \eqref{Eq: the primal deep hashing problem} into simpler structural blocks, enabling us to exploit the problem structure better. Building upon Problem~\eqref{Prop: block-wise regularized problem}, we develop a stochastic primal-dual algorithm {that addresses the lack of tractable proximal mappings in the nonsmooth composite form and the absence of convergence guarantees in existing deep hashing methods.}
