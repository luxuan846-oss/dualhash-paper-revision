\subsection{Related Work}
\label{Section: related work}
\paragraph{Optimization in Deep Hashing}
Existing approaches to bridging the continuous--discrete gap in deep hashing fall into three categories.
One line of work adopts continuous relaxation and introduces explicit quantization regularization on network outputs; in particular, W-type regularizers such as $\||\mathbf{u}|-\mathbf{1}\|_1$ have been widely used to encourage binary-like activations \cite{liuDeepSupervisedHashing,wang2023deep,zhuDeepHashingNetwork2016}. Another line refines the discretization objective through greedy coding schemes, central similarity targets, or proxy-based supervision \cite{su2018greedy,yuanCentralSimilarityQuantization2020,semanticHashCenters2025}. Alternative strategies directly address binary constraints by introducing auxiliary hash codes \cite{liDeepSupervisedHashing,li2017deep,liFeatureLearningBased2016}. Notably, even recent architectures retain W-type regularization; for instance, He and Wei \cite{he2025mambahash} employs $\||\mathbf{u}|-\mathbf{1}\|_2^2$ as a smooth surrogate for $\||\mathbf{u}|-\mathbf{1}\|_1$ to facilitate gradient-based optimization, indicating that the practical relevance of W-type regularization has not diminished. The remaining challenge is to develop theoretically grounded optimization methods that can directly handle nonsmooth W-type regularization under nonlinear deep network mappings, which is precisely the focus of this work.
% Recent studies have further strengthened backbone architectures to improve feature quality, extending deep hashing from conventional CNNs to hybrid CNN--Transformer frameworks \cite{he2024hybridhash}, Transformer-based hashing models \cite{chen2021transhash,wu2025fth,liu2025capmh}, and state-space architectures \cite{he2025mambahash}. 
\paragraph{Stochastic Nonconvex Constrained Optimization}
{From a constrained optimization perspective, Problem~\eqref{Eq: the general nonlinear composite problem in intro} can be reformulated as a nonlinearly constrained optimization problem of the form}
\[
  \min_{\bx \in \calX}\; f(\bx) + h(\bx) \quad \text{s.t.}\quad \bc(\bx) = 0.
\]{Several recent works have established oracle complexity results for first-order stochastic augmented-Lagrangian and penalty methods for problems of this type.} For instance, Jin and Wang \cite{jin2022stochastic} proposed a single-loop stochastic primal-dual method based on a linearized augmented-Lagrangian function, achieving oracle complexities of $\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-6})$ from an arbitrary initial point and $\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-5})$ from a feasible one for locating an $\varepsilon$-KKT point. By incorporating the recursive momentum estimator of \cite{cutkoskyMomentumBasedVarianceReduction2019}, Shi et al. \cite{shi2025momentum} developed a momentum-based linearized augmented-Lagrangian method, improving these bounds to $\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-4})$, or the nearly optimal $\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-3})$ when initialized at a nearly feasible point. To certify a stronger notion of stationarity that deterministically enforces constraint violation of at most $\varepsilon$, Lu et al. \cite{lu2024variancereducedfirstordermethodsdeterministically} introduced a linearized quadratic-penalty method with truncated momentum. Under a regularity condition on the constraint gradients, recursive momentum yields the nearly optimal complexity $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(\varepsilon^{-3})$. 
% while Polyak momentum with diminishing step sizes achieves $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(\varepsilon^{-4})$.
\paragraph{Primal-Dual Methods for Structured Composite Problems}
Primal-Dual Hybrid Gradient (PDHG)
\cite{chambolleFirstOrderPrimalDualAlgorithm2011}, as a foundational primal-dual method with ergodic $\mathcal{O}(1/k)$ convergence for convex problems, is widely used in imaging inverse problems.
Under convex setting, Esser et al. \cite{esserZhangChan2010} proposed a unified framework for a broad class of first-order primal-dual algorithms under the same convex setting. The stochastic extension SPDHG \cite{chambolleSPDHG2018} handles structured finite-sum objectives under arbitrary sampling, with almost-sure and ergodic $\mathcal{O}(1/k)$ guarantees. Chouzenoux et al. \cite{chouzenouxAdjointMismatch} further showed that primal-dual methods retain convergence under adjoint mismatch, while Chouzenoux and Pesquet \cite{chouzenouxStochasticMM2017} developed a stochastic majorize-minimize subspace algorithm for online penalized estimation in imaging reconstruction.
Extending to nonconvex settings, Clason et al. \cite{clasonAccelerationGlobalConvergence2019} established global convergence of a first-order primal-dual method under the Kurdyka--{\L}ojasiewicz condition. For nonconvex composite finite-sum objectives, Guo et al. \cite{guo2023preconditioned} proposed preconditioned primal-dual gradient methods with convergence analysis, while Tran-Dinh et al. \cite{tran2022hybrid} developed a hybrid stochastic framework achieving $\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-4})$ complexity. 
% When the problem admits a block-separable structure, ADMM provides a natural decomposition framework; nonconvex ADMM convergence has been systematically established \cite{li2015global,hong2016convergence,wangGlobalConvergenceADMM2018,boct2020proximal}, and Huang et al. \cite{huang2019faster} established an $\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-4})$ sample complexity bound for the nonconvex stochastic case. 
% {However, the above results either rely on convex or linearly coupled structures, or address only single-block stochastic updates; the multi-block, nonconvex, nonlinearly coupled stochastic setting arising in deep hashing remains largely unaddressed.}

Throughout this paper, $\bm{1}$ represents a vector of all ones, and $i, j$ serve as indices. Let $\calX, \calB, \calY$ be finite-dimensional real Hilbert spaces equipped with inner product $\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle$ and induced norm $\| \cdot \| := \sqrt{\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle}$. Unless specified otherwise, $\| \cdot \|$ denotes the Euclidean norm for vectors and the Frobenius norm for matrices. We use $\calX^{*}, \calB^{*}, \calY^{*}$ to represent the dual spaces. $|\cdot|$ denotes element-wise absolute value. For a closed set $\calC \subseteq \calX$, the distance from a point $\bx \in \calX$ to $\calC$ is defined as $\text{dist}(\bx, \mathcal{C}) := \min_{\by \in \mathcal{C}}\|\bx - \by\|$.
