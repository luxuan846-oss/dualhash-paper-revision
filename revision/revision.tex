\noindent Dear Editor-in-Chief,

We respectfully ask for reconsideration of the decision on our manuscript [Submission ID], "DualHash: A Stochastic Primal-Dual Algorithm with Theoretical Guarantee for Deep Hashing."

Our manuscript addresses a fundamental optimization challenge in deep hashing: quantization. We transform the nonconvex
W-regularized ($\|\,|z|-\mathbf{1}\|_1$) problem into a convex dual formulation with closed-form proximal updates via
Fenchel duality, and establish rigorous complexity guarantees complemented by extensive experiments that, as
both reviewers acknowledged, demonstrate the approach's practical potential. Specifically, deep hashing maps images to short binary codes (e.g., 64 bits) for large-scale retrieval, reducing storage by orders of magnitude and enabling sub-millisecond search via Hamming distance. The W-type regularizer is widely used in practice to enforce binarization, but its nonconvexity and nonsmoothness have left convergence and complexity analysis \textbf{largely unexplored} for W-regularized hashing methods, despite their strong empirical performance [].

However, we received the rejection decision and the reviewers' comments. We carefully considered all comments. While we appreciate the reviewers' and the Associate Editor's time and effort, we respectfully disagree with several points raised. We believe some concerns underlying the rejection decision stem from a misunderstanding of the manuscript's formulation and theoretical results. We believe some of the concerns underlying the rejection decision stem from a misunderstanding of the manuscript's formulation and theoretical results. Below, we clarify the reviewers' major concerns and present the revisions we made in response.

More importantly,  we have also obtained a result that we believe is directly relevant to SIIMS's scope: while it is well understood that using the conjugate function of the nonconvex function introduces a convex relaxation (M\"{o}llenhoff et al., SIIMS 2015), whether this relaxation preserves stationarity of the original model depends on the specific penalty. For the W-type regularizer $\|\,|z|-\mathbf{1}\|_1$, we establish
an explicit, verifiable necessary-and-sufficient condition for this transfer (see \red{[Section x]}for details), a model-specific characterization that, to the best of our knowledge, has not appeared in existing primal--dual analyses.

\section*{Reviewer 2, Comment 1: Stationarity Relationship}

\noindent\textbullet\quad\textbf{Reviewer comment.} \emph{%
The key question is whether this transformed problem provides meaningful guarantees for the original nonconvex W-regularized hashing objective. I do not find this connection convincingly established. In particular, the paper should clarify whether there is \blue{strong duality, zero duality gap, or \rigorousstationarity between the transformed primal--dual problem and the original objective.}}

\vspace{0.5em}
\noindent\textbullet\quad\textbf{\red{Our response.} }
We thank the reviewer for this question. We keep the notation of the
manuscript: Problem~(P) reads
\[
\tag{P}
\min_{x,B}\ J(x,B)=F(x,B)+h(B),\qquad
F(x,B)=f(x)+\frac{\gamma}{2n}\sum_{i=1}^n\|D_i(x)-b_i\|^2,\qquad
h(B)=\lambda\sum_{i,j}\bigl||B_{ij}|-1\bigr|.
\]
Problem~(D) is the dual associated with the splitting $Y=B$, i.e., with the Lagrangian
\[
\widehat{\mathcal{L}}(x,B,Y,\Lambda)=F(x,B)+h(Y)+\langle\Lambda,B-Y\rangle ,
\]
and eliminating $Y$ yields the reduced function with the Fenchel function $h^*$
\[
\mathcal{L}(x,B,\Lambda)=F(x,B)+\langle B,\Lambda\rangle-h^*(\Lambda).
\tag{4.2}
\]
Our complexity results (Theorems~5.2 and~5.3) target
\[
\operatorname{crit}\mathcal{L}
:=\bigl\{(x,B,\Lambda):\
0\in\partial\mathcal{L}(x,B,\Lambda)\bigr\},\quad\text{i.e.,}\quad
\nabla_xF=0,\quad
\nabla_BF+\Lambda=0,\quad
B\in\partial h^*(\Lambda),
\]
in the approximate sense: they bound the stochastic first-order oracle calls
needed to find a point satisfying
\[
\mathbb{E}\bigl[\operatorname{dist}^2\bigl(0,\,
\partial\mathcal{L}(x^k,B^k,\Lambda^k)\bigr)\bigr]
\le\varepsilon^2.
\tag{5.1}
\]
In the original manuscript, we have established that every zero-gap
primal--dual optimal pair lies in $\operatorname{crit}\mathcal{L}$ in \textbf{ Lemma~5.2 }; see (1).
We then show that a zero duality gap \textbf{may fail for the W-type regularized
problem} with a simple counterexample; see (2). And we add a necessary-and-sufficient condition under which a point in $\operatorname{crit}\mathcal{L}$ is stationary for Problem~(P) in (3).

\vspace{0.3em}
\noindent\textbf{(1) Strong duality: every zero-gap primal--dual pair lies in
$\operatorname{crit}\mathcal{L}$.}

Lemma~5.2 of the original manuscript treats the case in which strong duality
holds between (P) and (D). Its proof rests on the optimality system~(5.2).
The first equation states that $(x^*,B^*)$ minimizes
$\mathcal{L}(\cdot,\cdot,\Lambda^*)$; the second,
$h(B^*)+h^*(\Lambda^*)=\langle\Lambda^*,B^*\rangle$, is precisely the
zero-duality-gap condition. The first equation gives the first two conditions
in $\operatorname{crit}\mathcal{L}$. The second gives
$B^*\in\partial h^*(\Lambda^*)$, since for every $\Lambda$,
\[
h^*(\Lambda)\ \ge\ \langle\Lambda,B^*\rangle-h(B^*)
\ =\ h^*(\Lambda^*)+\langle B^*,\Lambda-\Lambda^*\rangle .
\]
Hence every saddle point of $\widehat{\mathcal{L}}$, i.e., every pair of
optimal solutions of (P) and (D) with zero duality gap, belongs to
$\operatorname{crit}\mathcal{L}$. Consequently, whenever such pairs exist,
the target set of our complexity analysis contains all of them.



\vspace{0.3em}
\noindent\textbf{(2) A zero duality gap can fail already in the simplest
instance of Problem~(P).}
\begin{example}\label{ex:gap}
Consider the one-dimensional instance
$\min_{b\in\mathbb{R}}\bigl\{\tfrac12 b^2+\bigl||b|-1\bigr|\bigr\}$,
i.e., $F(b)=\tfrac12 b^2$ and $\lambda=1$. The primal optimum is attained at
$b^*=\pm1$ with $p^*=\tfrac12$. Since
$\min_b\{\tfrac12 b^2+\Lambda b\}=-\tfrac12\Lambda^2$ and
$h^*(\Lambda)=|\Lambda|$ on $[-1,1]$,
\[
d^*=\sup_{|\Lambda|\le1}\Bigl\{-\tfrac12\Lambda^2-|\Lambda|\Bigr\}=0,
\]
attained at $\Lambda^*=0$. Hence $p^*-d^*=\tfrac12>0$: strong duality
between (P) and (D) fails, and no zero-gap primal--dual pair exists.
\end{example}

Example~\ref{ex:gap} shows that a zero duality gap cannot be expected for
this class of problems, even when $F$ is strongly convex: the gap stems from
the W-type regularizer itself. For such problems, first-order stationarity is
the natural target, as is standard for nonconvex primal--dual and splitting
methods such as ADMM and
PDHG~\cite{mollenhoff2015primal,li2015global,clason2019acceleration}.

\vspace{0.3em}
\noindent\textbf{(3) When a critical point of $\mathcal{L}$ is stationary
for Problem~(P).}

The revision adds the following result, where $\partial h$ denotes the
limiting subdifferential used throughout the manuscript.

\begin{proposition}[Stationarity transfer]\label{prop:transfer}
Let $(\tilde x,\widetilde B,\widetilde\Lambda)\in\operatorname{crit}\mathcal{L}$, i.e.,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:crit}
\nabla_xF(\tilde x,\widetilde B)=0,\qquad
\nabla_BF(\tilde x,\widetilde B)+\widetilde\Lambda=0,\qquad
\widetilde B\in\partial h^*(\widetilde\Lambda).
\end{equation}
Then the following statements are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item $(\tilde x,\widetilde B)$ is a stationary point of Problem~(P):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pstat}
\nabla_xF(\tilde x,\widetilde B)=0,\qquad
0\in\nabla_BF(\tilde x,\widetilde B)+\partial h(\widetilde B);
\end{equation}
\item the Fenchel--Young equality in~(5.2) holds at
$(\widetilde B,\widetilde\Lambda)$:
$h(\widetilde B)+h^*(\widetilde\Lambda)=\langle\widetilde\Lambda,\widetilde B\rangle$;
\item $|\widetilde B_{ij}|\ge1$ for all $i,j$;
\item $|D_{ij}(\tilde x)|\ge1$ for all $i,j$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

Condition~(b) is exactly the second equation of~(5.2) used in Lemma~5.2,
now required only at the limit point rather than together with global
optimality; Proposition~\ref{prop:transfer} is thus the local counterpart
of Lemma~5.2. The condition is imposed only at the stationary point, not
along the iterates, and it can be checked a posteriori from the network
outputs. In the form~(d), it states that all output entries lie outside
$(-1,1)$, which is precisely the binarization that the W-type regularizer
is designed to promote. The condition cannot be dropped: in
Example~\ref{ex:gap}, $(b,\Lambda)=(0,0)\in\operatorname{crit}\mathcal{L}$
violates~(c), and $b=0$ is not stationary for Problem~(P). The proof is
given in the Appendix.

\section*{Appendix: Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:transfer}}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 0: a consequence of \eqref{eq:pd-stationarity}.}
Since $h^{**}$ is proper, closed and convex, and $(h^{**})^*=h^*$, the subgradient inversion rule~\cite[Theorem~23.5]{rockafellar1970convex} gives
\[
\widetilde{B}\in\partial h^*(\widetilde{\Lambda})
\iff
\widetilde{\Lambda}\in\partial h^{**}(\widetilde{B})
\iff
h^{**}(\widetilde{B})+h^*(\widetilde{\Lambda})=\langle\widetilde{\Lambda},\widetilde{B}\rangle .
\tag{A.1}
\]
In addition, \eqref{eq:pd-stationarity} gives $\widetilde{\Lambda}=-\nabla_B F(\tilde{x},\widetilde{B})$, so \eqref{eq:original-stationarity} holds if and only if $\widetilde{\Lambda}\in\partial h(\widetilde{B})$.

\emph{(b)$\iff$(c).} By (A.1), the Fenchel--Young equality for $h$ holds at $(\widetilde{B},\widetilde{\Lambda})$ if and only if $h(\widetilde{B})=h^{**}(\widetilde{B})$. Comparing $h$ with $h^{**}(B)=\lambda\sum_{i,j}(|B_{ij}|-1)_+$ componentwise, this is equivalent to $|\widetilde{B}_{ij}|\ge1$ for all $i,j$.

\emph{(b)$\Rightarrow$(a).} The equality $h(\widetilde{B})+h^*(\widetilde{\Lambda})=\langle\widetilde{\Lambda},\widetilde{B}\rangle$ together with the definition of $h^*$ means that $\widetilde{B}$ globally minimizes $h(\cdot)-\langle\widetilde{\Lambda},\cdot\rangle$. By Fermat's rule~\cite[Theorem~10.1]{rockafellar1998variational} and Proposition~A.2 of the manuscript, $\widetilde{\Lambda}\in\widehat{\partial}h(\widetilde{B})\subseteq\partial h(\widetilde{B})$, which is \eqref{eq:original-stationarity}.

\emph{(a)$\Rightarrow$(c).} By Step~0, (a) means $\widetilde{\Lambda}\in\partial h(\widetilde{B})\cap\partial h^{**}(\widetilde{B})$. Both functions are separable, so it suffices to examine a scalar component $b=\widetilde{B}_{ij}$. If $|b|<1$, then $\partial h^{**}(b)=\{0\}$, whereas $\partial h(b)=\{-\lambda\,\mathrm{sgn}(b)\}$ for $b\neq0$ and $\partial h(0)=\{-\lambda,\lambda\}$; in all cases $0\notin\partial h(b)$, so the intersection is empty. Hence $|\widetilde{B}_{ij}|\ge1$ for all $i,j$.
\end{proof}
