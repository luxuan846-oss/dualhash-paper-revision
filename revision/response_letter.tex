To address the comments in the decision letter and the referees' reports, we have strengthened the paper in three main respects:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \textbf{Building on the zero-gap result of the submitted version, we establish the stationarity relationship between the transformed primal--dual problem and the original objective without assuming zero duality gap, which addresses the main question raised by Referee~\#1.} In particular, we characterize when KKT points transfer to the penalty model and show that the residual of the original objective is bounded by the optimization error and the quantization error, which links the quality of the final binary codes to the optimization guarantee.
\item \textbf{Whereas the earlier analysis focused on the $\ell_1$ regularizer, we now formally define the general class of W-type regularizers and clarify the problem formulations and notation, as suggested by Referee~\#1.} From their common structural conditions, we derive the convex conjugates in unified form and specify the scope of each theoretical result.
\item \textbf{Following the detailed comments of Referee~\#2, the convergence analysis retains the framework and the complexity orders of the submitted manuscript, with refined assumptions and expectation estimates.} Specifically, we refine the lower-boundedness assumption to justify the coercivity of the objective, make the lower bounds of the potentials explicit, and correct the conditional-expectation and variance estimates.
\end{enumerate}

Following these revisions, we have updated the title from ``DualHash: A Stochastic Primal-Dual Algorithm with Theoretical Guarantee for Deep Hashing'' to ``DualHash: Stochastic Primal-Dual \blue{Algorithms} for \blue{W-Type Regularized} Deep Hashing'' to better reflect the content of the paper. {\Large\bfseries Response to referee \#1\par}
\end{center}

\medskip
{\itshape The paper presents DualHash, a primal-dual reformulation for deep hashing with W-type quantization regularization. My understanding is that the main contribution is an adaptation of primal-dual splitting, combined with stochastic momentum/STORM updates, to deep hashing. The method uses the Fenchel conjugate of a specific W-type regularizer to obtain a closed-form dual proximal update.}

\medskip
\noindent{\large\bfseries Comments:}
\begin{qalist}
\item[{Q1.}] {\itshape However, the convexity of the dual/conjugate side is standard. The key question is whether this transformed problem provides meaningful guarantees for the original nonconvex W-regularized hashing objective. I do not find this connection convincingly established. In particular, the paper should clarify whether there is strong duality, zero duality gap, or at least a rigorous stationarity relationship between the transformed primal-dual problem and the original objective.}
\item[Response:]
We sincerely appreciate this thoughtful comment, which helped us improve our optimality analysis. \newred{In the submitted manuscript (Lemma~5.2), we showed} that, under zero duality gap, the optimal primal--dual pair is a KKT point of the Lagrangian $\calL$. Following \newred{your} comment, \newred{we show that strong duality and zero duality gap can fail for W-type regularizers, but a stationarity relationship with the original W-regularized hashing objective still holds. We make this precise in three results below.}

The problems under consideration and the Lagrangian in our manuscript are given by:
\begin{align*}
&\min_{\bx}\ J_0(\bx):=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n f_i(\bx)+h(\calD(\bx)),\tag{P0}\\
&\min_{\bx,\bB}\ J(\bx,\bB):=F(\bx,\bB)+h(\bB),\tag{P}\\
&\max_{\bLmbd}\min_{\bx,\bB}\ \calL(\bx,\bB,\bLmbd):=F(\bx,\bB)+\langle\bB,\bLmbd\rangle-h^*(\bLmbd),\tag{D}
\end{align*}
where \newred{(P0) is the original W-regularized hashing problem in the submitted manuscript, and} $\bB=(b_{ij})\in\RR^{n\times d}$. Here, our algorithms target an $\varepsilon$-KKT point of $\calL$, satisfying $\EE\big[\dist^2(\zero,\calK(\bx,\bB,\bLmbd))\big]\le\varepsilon^2$ for the KKT mapping
\[
\calK(\bx,\bB,\bLmbd):=\big(\nabla_{\bx}F(\bx,\bB),\ \nabla_{\bB}F(\bx,\bB)+\bLmbd,\ \bB-\partial h^*(\bLmbd)\big).
\]
\textbf{1.~Zero duality gap and strong duality are equivalent for Problem~(P), but the gap can be positive for W-type regularizers.} Since Problems~(P) and~(D) both admit optimal solutions under our problem setting and assumptions, the two notions coincide. For W-type regularizers, however, the gap can be positive even with a convex $F$. We have added Example~\mref{ex:gap} \blue{(\mpl{ex:gap})} to show this for $h(b)=\bigl||b|-1\bigr|$:
\begin{bluequote}
\textbf{Example~\mref{ex:gap}.} Consider the problem
\[
p^*=\min_{b\in\RR}\Bigl\{\tfrac12 b^2+\bigl||b|-1\bigr|\Bigr\}.
\]
Its optimal value is $p^*=\tfrac12$, attained at $b^*=\pm1$. The dual value is
\[
d^*=\sup_{|\Lambda|\le1}\Bigl\{-\tfrac12\Lambda^2-|\Lambda|\Bigr\}=0,
\]
attained at $\Lambda^*=0$. Hence $p^*-d^*=\tfrac12>0$, and no zero-gap primal--dual pair exists.
\end{bluequote}
While the submitted manuscript discussed the zero-gap case, this example shows that a KKT point of $\calL$ need not be stationary for Problem~(P). This motivates us to characterize the stationarity condition through the conjugate structure of W-type regularizers.

\textbf{2.~A KKT point of $\calL$ is stationary for Problem~(P) if and only if $|\tilde b_{ij}|\ge1$ for all $i,j$.} Let $h$ be a W-type regularizer. A point $(\tilde{\bx},\tilde{\bB})$ is a stationary point for Problem~(P) if
\[
\nabla_{\bx}F(\tilde{\bx},\tilde{\bB})=\zero,
\qquad
-\nabla_{\bB}F(\tilde{\bx},\tilde{\bB})\in\partial h(\tilde{\bB}).
\]
In contrast, a KKT point $(\tilde{\bx},\tilde{\bB},\tilde{\bLmbd})$ satisfies
\[
\nabla_{\bx}F(\tilde{\bx},\tilde{\bB})=\zero,
\qquad
-\nabla_{\bB}F(\tilde{\bx},\tilde{\bB})\in\partial h^{**}(\tilde{\bB}),
\]
where the second condition follows from $\tilde{\bB}\in\partial h^*(\tilde{\bLmbd})$ and $h^*=(h^{**})^*$, with $h^{**}$ the biconjugate of $h$. Although $h$ is nonconvex, \newred{the KKT condition implies stationarity} whenever
\[
h(\tilde{\bB})=h^{**}(\tilde{\bB}).
\tag{R1.1}\label{eq:r1-hull}
\]
For the $\ell_1$ W-regularizer $h(\bB)=\lambda\lVert|\bB|-\mathbf{1}\rVert_1$, we have $h^{**}(\bB)=\lambda\lVert(|\bB|-\mathbf{1})_+\rVert_1$, so \eqref{eq:r1-hull} becomes $|\tilde b_{ij}|\ge1$ for all $i,j$. This condition is also necessary, since for $|b|<1$ the componentwise subdifferentials satisfy $\partial h^{**}(b)=\{0\}$ and $0\notin\partial h(b)$. We have formally stated this necessary and sufficient condition for all W-type regularizers as Proposition~\mref{prop:transfer} \blue{(\mpl{prop:transfer})}:
\begin{bluequote}
\textbf{Proposition~\mref{prop:transfer}.} Let $h$ be a W-type regularizer, and let $(\tilde{\bx},\tilde{\bB},\tilde{\bLmbd})$ be a KKT point of $\calL$. The following statements are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)},leftmargin=2em,before=\let\item\reviewitem]
\item $(\tilde{\bx},\tilde{\bB})$ is a stationary point of Problem~(P), i.e., $\nabla_{\bx}F(\tilde{\bx},\tilde{\bB})=\zero$ and $\zero\in\nabla_{\bB}F(\tilde{\bx},\tilde{\bB})+\partial h(\tilde{\bB})$;
\item $h(\tilde{\bB})+h^*(\tilde{\bLmbd})=\langle\tilde{\bLmbd},\tilde{\bB}\rangle$;
\item $|\tilde b_{ij}|\ge1$ for all $i,j$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{bluequote}

\textbf{3.~An approximate KKT point is \newred{near-stationary for} Problem~(P0), up to an error controlled by the quantization error.} Since Problem~(P0) is nonconvex and nonsmooth, we now adopt the following standard notion~\cite{davis2019,tian2022stationarity} to measure its stationarity \blue{(\mpl{def:near-stationarity})}:
\begin{bluequote}
\textbf{Definition~\mref{def:near-stationarity} (Near-stationarity).} Given $\delta,\varepsilon\ge0$, a point $\bx$ is called \emph{$(\delta,\varepsilon)$-near-stationary} for Problem~(P0) if there exists $\widehat{\bx}$ such that
\[
\|\widehat{\bx}-\bx\|\le\delta,
\qquad
\dist\bigl(\zero,\partial J_0(\widehat{\bx})\bigr)\le\varepsilon,
\]
where $\partial$ denotes the limiting subdifferential.
\end{bluequote}
We have also formally defined the quantization error in deep hashing.
\[
Q_1(\bx):=\|\calD(\bx)-\operatorname{sgn}(\calD(\bx))\|_1.
\]
We establish that under our existing assumptions, for any $(\bx,\bB,\bLmbd)$ with KKT residual $r_{\calK}:=\dist(\zero,\calK(\bx,\bB,\bLmbd))$, there exists $\widehat{\bx}$ such that
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\|\widehat{\bx}-\bx\|
&\le\sqrt{\frac{2}{L}}\Bigl(\sqrt{\kappa}\,r_{\calK}+\sqrt{h(\calD(\bx))}\Bigr),\\
\dist\bigl(\zero,\partial J_0(\widehat{\bx})\bigr)
&\le\underbrace{\Bigl(\sqrt{1+G_{\calD}^2}+2\sqrt{2\kappa L}\Bigr)r_{\calK}}_{\text{optimization error}}
+\underbrace{2\sqrt{2L\,h(\calD(\bx))}}_{\text{structural error}},
\end{aligned}
\tag{R1.2}\label{eq:r1-cert}
\end{equation}
where \newred{$\kappa$, $L$, and $G_{\calD}$ are problem-dependent constants.} The structural error is governed by the regularizer value $h(\calD(\bx))$, which equals $\lambda Q_1(\bx)$ for the $\ell_1$ W-regularizer. Consequently, combining \eqref{eq:r1-cert} with the oracle complexities established in the submitted manuscript, DualHash-StoM and DualHash-StoRM find \newred{an output $\bx^R$} that is $\calO(\varepsilon)$-near-stationary for Problem~(P0) in expectation with $\calO(\varepsilon^{-4})$ and $\calO(\varepsilon^{-3})$ SFO calls, respectively, provided that its quantization error satisfies $\lambda\EE\,Q_1(\bx^R)=\calO(\varepsilon^2)$. 

We have formally stated these results as Theorem~\mref{prop:original-certificate} (\mpl{prop:original-certificate}) and Corollary~\mref{cor:original-stationarity} (\mpl{cor:original-stationarity}):
\begin{bluequote}
\textbf{Theorem~\mref{prop:original-certificate}.} Under Assumption~\mref{Ass: basic regularity}(ii), let $h$ be a W-type regularizer defined in Definition~\mref{def:wtype}, let $\kappa:=(1+m^2)/(4m)$, and let $L>0$ satisfy $L\ge L_f+\|\bLmbd\|L_{\nabla\calD}$. Then, for any $(\bx,\bB,\bLmbd)$ satisfying $r_{\calK}<\infty$, there exists $\widehat{\bx}\in\RR^q$ such that
\begin{align*}
\|\widehat{\bx}-\bx\|
&\le\sqrt{\frac{2}{L}}\Bigl(\sqrt{\kappa}\,r_{\calK}+\sqrt{h(\calD(\bx))}\Bigr),\\
\dist\bigl(\zero,\partial J_0(\widehat{\bx})\bigr)
&\le\underbrace{\Bigl(\sqrt{1+G_{\calD}^2}+2\sqrt{2\kappa L}\Bigr)r_{\calK}}_{\text{optimization error}}
+\underbrace{2\sqrt{2L\,h(\calD(\bx))}}_{\text{structural error}}.
\end{align*}
If $\operatorname{dom}\phi^*\subseteq[-\lambda,\lambda]$, then $\|\bLmbd\|\le\lambda\sqrt{nd}$, and one can take $L=L_0:=L_f+\lambda\sqrt{nd}\,L_{\nabla\calD}$, independent of $\bLmbd$.
\end{bluequote}
\begin{bluequote}
\textbf{Corollary~\mref{cor:original-stationarity}.} Under the assumptions of Theorem~\mref{Theorem: the oracle complexity in SGDM} (resp.\ Theorem~\mref{Theorem: the oracle complexity in STORM}), let $(\bx^R,\bB^R,\bLmbd^R)$ be the output of Algorithm~\mref{alg:our_algorithm_sgdm} (resp.\ Algorithm~\mref{alg:our_algorithm_storm}), obtained with $\calO(\varepsilon^{-4})$ (resp.\ $\calO(\varepsilon^{-3})$) SFO calls. Then $\bx^R$ is $(\delta_R,\varepsilon_R)$-near-stationary for Problem~(P0), where $\delta_R$ and $\varepsilon_R$ are the bounds of Theorem~\mref{prop:original-certificate} at $(\bx^R,\bB^R,\bLmbd^R)$ with $L=L_0$, and satisfy
\begin{align*}
\EE\,\delta_R &\le\sqrt{\frac{2}{L_0}}\Bigl(\sqrt{\kappa}\,\varepsilon+\sqrt{\EE\,h\bigl(\calD(\bx^R)\bigr)}\Bigr),\\
\EE\,\varepsilon_R &\le\Bigl(\sqrt{1+G_{\calD}^2}+2\sqrt{2\kappa L_0}\Bigr)\varepsilon+2\sqrt{2L_0\,\EE\,h\bigl(\calD(\bx^R)\bigr)},
\end{align*}
with $\kappa$ and $L_0$ given in Theorem~\mref{prop:original-certificate}.
\end{bluequote}

\item[{Q2.}] {\itshape I also found the setting and writing unclear. The term ``W-type regularizer'' is used repeatedly but never properly defined; only one example is given. Even by the start of Section 3, the precise problem being studied is still not fully clear. If ``W-type'' denotes a class of regularizers, that class should be formally specified. If it refers only to the particular regularizer $h(z) = \lambda \lVert |z| - 1 \rVert_1$, the paper should state this much earlier and avoid presenting the results as applying to a broader family.}
\item[Response:]
We thank the reviewer for this important comment, which helped us state the scope of our results precisely. \newred{The W-type regularizers form a class,} as the abstract and introduction of the submitted manuscript indicate, but we agree that the class was not formally defined. In the revision, Definition~\mref{def:wtype} specifies the class by conditions (W1)--(W3), and Table~\mref{tab:wtype} lists representative examples \blue{(\mpl{tab:wtype})}. \newred{We reproduce} the definition \blue{(\mpl{def:wtype})}:
\begin{bluequote}
\textbf{Definition~\mref{def:wtype} (W-type regularizer).} Let $\phi:\RR\to[0,+\infty)$. For $\bz\in\RR^{d}$ and $\bZ\in\RR^{n\times d}$, define $h(\bz):=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\phi(z_j)$ and $h(\bZ):=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\phi(z_{ij})$; with a slight abuse of notation, $h$, $h^*$, and $h^{**}$ refer to both the vector and the matrix cases. We call $h$ a \emph{W-type regularizer} if $\phi$ satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(W\arabic*),leftmargin=3em,before=\let\item\reviewitem]
\item $\phi$ is even and continuous, and $\phi(t)=0$ if and only if $t\in\{-1,1\}$;
\item $\phi$ is convex on $[1,+\infty)$;
\item $\phi$ decreases at a nonvanishing rate on $[0,1)$: for every $\theta\in(0,1)$ there exists $c_{\theta}>0$ such that $\phi(s)-\phi(t)\ge c_{\theta}(t-s)$ for all $0\le s<t\le\theta$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{bluequote}
