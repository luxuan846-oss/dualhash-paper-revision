\noindent Dear Editor-in-Chief,

We respectfully ask for reconsideration of the decision on our manuscript \#M187198, "DualHash: A Stochastic Primal-Dual Algorithm with Theoretical Guarantee for Deep Hashing."

Deep hashing maps images to short binary codes (e.g., 64 bits) for large-scale retrieval, reducing storage by orders of magnitude and enabling sub-millisecond search via Hamming distance. The W-type regularizer $\|\,|z|-\mathbf{1}\|_1$ is the principal penalty used in practice to enforce binarization, yet its nonconvexity and nonsmoothness have left convergence and complexity analysis largely unexplored for W-regularized hashing methods~\cite{liu2016dsh,zhu2016dhn,wang2023mds,chen2025shc}. Our manuscript addresses this gap: we reformulate the problem via Fenchel duality into a convex dual with closed-form proximal updates, and establish the first complexity guarantees for this setting, complemented by extensive experiments whose practical value both reviewers acknowledged.

However, we have received the rejection decision along with the reviewers' insightful comments. After carefully considering all feedback, and while we sincerely appreciate the time and effort of the reviewers and the Associate Editor, we respectfully disagree with several of the points raised. We believe some of the concerns underlying the rejection decision stem from a misunderstanding of the manuscript's formulation and theoretical results. Below, we clarify the reviewers' major concerns and present the revisions we made in response.

More importantly, we have also obtained a result that we believe is directly relevant to SIIMS's scope: while it is well understood that using the conjugate function of the nonconvex function introduces a convex relaxation~\cite{mollenhoff2015primal}, whether this relaxation preserves stationarity of the original model depends on the specific penalty. For the W-type regularizer $\|\,|z|-\mathbf{1}\|_1$, we establish an explicit, verifiable necessary-and-sufficient condition for this transfer (see Proposition~\ref{prop:transfer} below for details), \red{a model-specific characterization that, to the best of our knowledge, has not appeared in existing primal--dual analyses.}

\section{Reviewer 2, Comment 1: Stationarity Relationship}

\noindent\textbullet\quad\textbf{Reviewer comment.} \emph{%
The key question is whether this transformed problem provides meaningful guarantees for the original nonconvex W-regularized hashing objective. I do not find this connection convincingly established. In particular, the paper should clarify whether there is \blue{strong duality, zero duality gap, or \rigorousstationarity between the transformed primal--dual problem and the original objective.}}

\vspace{0.5em}
\noindent\textbullet\quad\textbf{\red{Our response.} }
We thank the reviewer for this question. We keep the notation of the
manuscript: Problem~(P) reads
\[
\tag{P}
\min_{x,B}\ J(x,B)=F(x,B)+h(B),\qquad
F(x,B)=f(x)+\frac{\gamma}{2n}\sum_{i=1}^n\|D_i(x)-b_i\|^2,\qquad
h(B)=\lambda\sum_{i,j}\bigl||B_{ij}|-1\bigr|.
\]

The dual problem~(D) is obtained by introducing the splitting $Y=B$
and eliminating $Y$ via the Fenchel conjugate~$h^*$:
\[
\max_{\Lambda}\min_{x,B}\
\mathcal{L}(x,B,\Lambda),\qquad
\mathcal{L}(x,B,\Lambda)
:=F(x,B)+\langle B,\Lambda\rangle-h^*(\Lambda).
\tag{D}
\]

Our complexity results (Theorems~5.2 and~5.3) target the critical set
\[
\operatorname{crit}\mathcal{L}
:=\bigl\{(x,B,\Lambda):\
0\in\partial\mathcal{L}(x,B,\Lambda)\bigr\},\quad\text{i.e.,}\quad
\nabla_xF=0,\quad
\nabla_BF+\Lambda=0,\quad
B\in\partial h^*(\Lambda),
\]
bounding the stochastic first-order oracle calls needed to
approximately reach it.

In the original manuscript, we have established that \textbf{every zero-gap
primal--dual optimal pair} lies in $\operatorname{crit}\mathcal{L}$ in \textbf{ Lemma~5.2 }; see (1).
We then show that a zero duality gap \textbf{may fail for the W-type regularized
problem} with a simple counterexample; see (2). And we add a necessary-and-sufficient condition under which a point in $\operatorname{crit}\mathcal{L}$ is stationary for Problem~(P) in (3).

\vspace{0.3em}
\noindent\textbf{(1) Zero-gap primal--dual pairs are covered
by the original manuscript.}

Lemma~5.2 of the submitted manuscript establishes: if
$(x^*,B^*,\Lambda^*)$ satisfies the optimality system
\[
(x^*,B^*)\in\arg\min_{x,B}\mathcal{L}(x,B,\Lambda^*),\qquad
h(B^*)+h^*(\Lambda^*)=\langle\Lambda^*,B^*\rangle,
\tag{5.2}
\]
then $(x^*,B^*,\Lambda^*)\in\operatorname{crit}\mathcal{L}$.
That is, whenever a zero-gap primal--dual optimal pair exists,
it belongs to the target set of our complexity analysis.


\vspace{0.3em}
\noindent\textbf{(2) A zero duality gap can fail already in
the simplest instance of Problem~(P).}

While Lemma~5.2 covers the zero-gap case, such a gap is not
guaranteed when $h$ is nonconvex. The following example shows
that it can fail even when $F$ is strongly convex.

\begin{example}\label{ex:gap}
Consider the one-dimensional instance
$\min_{b\in\mathbb{R}}\bigl\{\tfrac12 b^2+\bigl||b|-1\bigr|\bigr\}$,
i.e., $F(b)=\tfrac12 b^2$ and $\lambda=1$. The primal optimum is attained at
$b^*=\pm1$ with $p^*=\tfrac12$. Since
$\min_b\{\tfrac12 b^2+\Lambda b\}=-\tfrac12\Lambda^2$ and
$h^*(\Lambda)=|\Lambda|$ on $[-1,1]$,
\[
d^*=\sup_{|\Lambda|\le1}\Bigl\{-\tfrac12\Lambda^2-|\Lambda|\Bigr\}=0,
\]
attained at $\Lambda^*=0$. Hence $p^*-d^*=\tfrac12>0$: strong duality
between Problem~(P) and Problem~(D) fails, and no zero-gap primal--dual pair exists.
\end{example}

In the nonconvex setting, saddle points of the Lagrangian may
not exist, and first-order stationarity is the standard
convergence target in nonconvex primal--dual and splitting
methods. Representative results include ADMM for nonconvex
composite
problems~\cite{li2015global,hong2016convergence}, nonconvex
and nonsmooth ADMM for imaging
applications~\cite{yang2017admm,zhu2024inexact}, and
primal--dual methods for nonconvex problems with nonlinear
operators~\cite{clason2019acceleration}. In each case, the
convergence guarantee targets a stationary or KKT point, not
a saddle point or a globally optimal zero-gap pair.

Nevertheless, the reviewer's question highlights an important
point: what does stationarity of the reformulated
problem~\textup{(D)} imply for the original
problem~\textup{(P)}? We appreciate this question, which
prompted us to establish the following explicit
characterization (proved in the Appendix).

\vspace{0.3em}
\noindent\textbf{(3) New Proposition: stationarity transfer from
$\operatorname{crit}\mathcal{L}$ to Problem~(P).}

\begin{proposition}[Stationarity transfer]\label{prop:transfer}
Let $(\tilde x,\widetilde B,\widetilde\Lambda)\in\operatorname{crit}\mathcal{L}$, i.e.,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:crit}
\nabla_xF(\tilde x,\widetilde B)=0,\qquad
\nabla_BF(\tilde x,\widetilde B)+\widetilde\Lambda=0,\qquad
\widetilde B\in\partial h^*(\widetilde\Lambda).
\end{equation}
Then the following statements are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item $(\tilde x,\widetilde B)$ is a stationary point of Problem~(P):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pstat}
\nabla_xF(\tilde x,\widetilde B)=0,\qquad
0\in\nabla_BF(\tilde x,\widetilde B)+\partial h(\widetilde B);
\end{equation}
\item the Fenchel--Young equality in~(5.2) holds at
$(\widetilde B,\widetilde\Lambda)$:
$h(\widetilde B)+h^*(\widetilde\Lambda)=\langle\widetilde\Lambda,\widetilde B\rangle$;
\item $|\widetilde B_{ij}|\ge1$ for all $i,j$;
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

% Condition~(b) is the Fenchel--Young equality, the same
% equation used in Lemma~5.2 to treat the zero-gap case. The difference is that Proposition~\ref{prop:transfer}
% requires it only at the limit point, not along the entire
% iterate sequence.
% Condition~(c) can be checked a posteriori from the network
% outputs, which is precisely the binarization that the W-type
% regularizer is designed to promote.

That $h(B)=h^{**}(B)$ suffices for stationarity transfer is a
general fact from convex analysis, and the convex-relaxation
effect of conjugating a nonconvex penalty has been
recognized~\cite{mollenhoff2015primal}. However, an explicit
characterization of when this condition holds for a specific
penalty---and whether it is also necessary---has not been
provided: M\"{o}llenhoff et al.\ address the relaxation by
retaining the primal nonconvex term rather than
characterizing when it is exact;
Guo~\cite{guo2026preconditioned} targets a reduced system
involving $\partial h^*$ but does not give a penalty-specific
criterion. Proposition~\ref{prop:transfer} fills this gap for
the W-type regularizer, providing the verifiable threshold
$|\widetilde{B}_{ij}|\ge1$ as a necessary and sufficient
condition.

\section*{Appendix: Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:transfer}}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 0: a consequence of \eqref{eq:crit}.}
Since $h^{**}$ is proper, closed and convex, and $(h^{**})^*=h^*$, the subgradient inversion rule~\cite[Theorem~23.5]{rockafellar1970convex} gives
\[
\widetilde{B}\in\partial h^*(\widetilde{\Lambda})
\iff
\widetilde{\Lambda}\in\partial h^{**}(\widetilde{B})
\iff
h^{**}(\widetilde{B})+h^*(\widetilde{\Lambda})=\langle\widetilde{\Lambda},\widetilde{B}\rangle .
\tag{A.1}
\]
In addition, \eqref{eq:crit} gives $\widetilde{\Lambda}=-\nabla_B F(\tilde{x},\widetilde{B})$, so \eqref{eq:pstat} holds if and only if $\widetilde{\Lambda}\in\partial h(\widetilde{B})$.

\emph{(b)$\iff$(c).} By (A.1), the Fenchel--Young equality for $h$ holds at $(\widetilde{B},\widetilde{\Lambda})$ if and only if $h(\widetilde{B})=h^{**}(\widetilde{B})$. Comparing $h$ with $h^{**}(B)=\lambda\sum_{i,j}(|B_{ij}|-1)_+$ componentwise, this is equivalent to $|\widetilde{B}_{ij}|\ge1$ for all $i,j$.

\emph{(b)$\Rightarrow$(a).} The equality $h(\widetilde{B})+h^*(\widetilde{\Lambda})=\langle\widetilde{\Lambda},\widetilde{B}\rangle$ together with the definition of $h^*$ means that $\widetilde{B}$ globally minimizes $h(\cdot)-\langle\widetilde{\Lambda},\cdot\rangle$. By Fermat's rule~\cite[Theorem~10.1]{rockafellar1998variational} and Proposition~A.2 of the manuscript, $\widetilde{\Lambda}\in\widehat{\partial}h(\widetilde{B})\subseteq\partial h(\widetilde{B})$, which is \eqref{eq:pstat}.

\emph{(a)$\Rightarrow$(c).} By Step~0, (a) means $\widetilde{\Lambda}\in\partial h(\widetilde{B})\cap\partial h^{**}(\widetilde{B})$. Both functions are separable, so it suffices to examine a scalar component $b=\widetilde{B}_{ij}$. If $|b|<1$, then $\partial h^{**}(b)=\{0\}$, whereas $\partial h(b)=\{-\lambda\,\mathrm{sgn}(b)\}$ for $b\neq0$ and $\partial h(0)=\{-\lambda,\lambda\}$; in all cases $0\notin\partial h(b)$, so the intersection is empty. Hence $|\widetilde{B}_{ij}|\ge1$ for all $i,j$.
\end{proof}
