We are writing to respectfully ask for your reconsideration of the decision on our manuscript \textbf{[sumbmission ID]}, ``DualHash: A Stochastic Primal-Dual Algorithm with Theoretical Guarantee for Deep Hashing.''

The central contribution of this work is to address the
fundamental quantization difficulty in deep hashing through
Fenchel duality, accompanied by rigorous oracle complexity

\section*{Reviewer 2, Comment 1: Stationarity Relationship}

\noindent\textbullet\quad\textbf{Reviewer comment.} \emph{%
The key question is whether this transformed problem provides meaningful guarantees for the original nonconvex W-regularized hashing objective. I do not find this connection convincingly established. In particular, the paper should clarify whether there is \blue{strong duality, zero duality gap, or \rigorousstationarity between the transformed primal--dual problem and the original objective.}}

\vspace{0.5em}

\noindent\textbf{Our response.} 

We thank the reviewer for the opportunity to clarify this first-order stationarity relationship.

\textbf{(1) We establish that dual stationarity implies primal stationarity, and conversely, under the condition $|\widetilde{B}_{ij}|\ge 1$ for all $i,j$ in our W-regularized problem.}

Following the notation of our manuscript, the primal problem is
\[
\tag{P}
\min_{x,B} J(x,B) := F(x,B)+r(B),
\]
where $D_i$ is the output of the neural network and $F(x,B)=f(x)+\frac{\gamma}{2n}\sum_{i=1}^n \|D_i(x)-b_i\|^2$, and
\[
r(B)
=
\mu\sum_{i=1}^n\sum_{j=1}^d
\bigl||B_{ij}|-1\bigr|
\]
is the W-type regularizer. The corresponding Lagrangian is
\[
\mathcal{L}(x,B,\lambda)
:=
F(x,B)+\langle \lambda,B\rangle-r^*(\lambda),
\]
and the dual problem is
\[
\tag{D}
\max_{\lambda}\;\min_{x,B}\;
\mathcal{L}(x,B,\lambda).
\]

We establish the following relationship between formulation~(D) and Problem~(P).

\begin{proposition}[Stationarity transfer]
Suppose $(\tilde{x},\tilde{B},\tilde{\lambda})$ satisfies the first-order stationarity conditions of~(D):
\[
\tag{D-stat}\label{eq:pd-stationarity}
\begin{cases}
\nabla_x F(\tilde{x},\tilde{B})=0,\\[4pt]
\nabla_B F(\tilde{x},\tilde{B})+\tilde{\lambda}=0,\\[4pt]
\tilde{B}\in\partial r^*(\tilde{\lambda}).
\end{cases}
\]
Then the following statements are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $(\tilde{x},\tilde{B})$ is a first-order stationary point of Problem~(P), i.e.,
\[
\tag{P-stat}\label{eq:original-stationarity}
\begin{cases}
\nabla_x F(\tilde{x},\tilde{B})=0,\\[4pt]
0\in\nabla_B F(\tilde{x},\tilde{B})+\partial r(\tilde{B});
\end{cases}
\]
\item the contact condition holds:
\[
r(\tilde{B})=r^{**}(\tilde{B})
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
|\tilde{B}_{ij}|\ge 1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
|D_{ij}(\tilde{x})|\ge 1,
\qquad\text{for all } i,j.
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}



\vspace{0.3em}

\textbf{(2) We show that the contact condition
$|B_{ij}^*|\ge 1$ is necessary for our nonconvex regularized problem: without it, stationarity
transfer fails and a positive duality gap arises.}

\vspace{0.3em}

\begin{example}
Consider the one-dimensional problem
\[
\min_{b\in\mathbb{R}}
\left\{
\frac{1}{2}b^2
+\bigl||b|-1\bigr|
\right\}.
\]
The primal optimum is attained at $b^*=\pm 1$ with
$p^*=\tfrac{1}{2}$. The corresponding dual optimum is
\[
d^*
=
\sup_{|\lambda|\le 1}
\left\{
-\tfrac{1}{2}\lambda^2-|\lambda|
\right\}
=0,
\]
attained at $\lambda^*=0$. Thus $p^*-d^*=\tfrac{1}{2}>0$,
and strong duality fails.

Moreover, the triple $(b^*,\lambda^*)=(0,0)$ satisfies
the transformed first-order system:
\[
F'(0)+\lambda^*=0,
\qquad
b^*=0\in\partial r^*(0)=[-1,1].
\]
But $0\notin\partial r(0)=\{-1,1\}$, so stationarity
for the original objective fails at this point. The
contact condition also fails: $|b^*|=0<1$.
\end{example}

\noindent This confirms that the equivalence established in~(1)
is sharp. The contact condition is the precise boundary
between success and failure of stationarity transfer for
the W-type regularizer.

\textbf{(3) While strong duality
cannot be guaranteed under the present assumptions, the established approach in nonconvex primal--dual optimization is to characterize stationarity relationships, which is what our Proposition
provides.}

\vspace{0.3em}

While the counterexample in~(2) rules out both zero
duality gap and unconditional stationarity transfer for
the W-type regularizer in nonconvex problem. this is consistent with the broader
nonconvex optimization literature, where primal--dual
methods do not assume strong duality but instead
establish explicit stationarity relationships between
the formulations involved~\cite{Bolte2018,li2015global}.

\section*{Appendix: Proof of Proposition~1}

\begin{proof}
Recalling the definition of the W-type regularizer and its biconjugate, we have

$$
r(B)=\mu\sum_{i,j}\bigl||B_{ij}|-1\bigr|,
\qquad
r^{**}(B)=\mu\sum_{i,j}(|B_{ij}|-1)_+,
$$ implies
$$
r(\tilde{B})=r^{**}(\tilde{B})
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
|\tilde{B}_{ij}|\ge1
\quad\text{for all }i,j.
$$

It remains to relate the contact condition to stationarity. Since

$$
\tilde{B}\in\partial r^*(\tilde{\lambda})
$$

and \(r^*=(r^{**})^*\), Fenchel conjugacy gives

$$
\tilde{B}\in\partial r^*(\tilde{\lambda})
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\tilde{\lambda}\in\partial r^{**}(\tilde{B}).
$$

On the other hand, the second condition of
\eqref{eq:pd-stationarity} gives

$$
\tilde{\lambda}=-\nabla_BF(\tilde{x},\tilde{B}).
$$

Hence, \((\tilde{x},\tilde{B})\) satisfies
\eqref{eq:original-stationarity} if and only if
$$
\tilde{\lambda}\in\partial r(\tilde{B}).
$$

For the W-type regularizer, under
\(\tilde{B}\in\partial r^*(\tilde{\lambda})\), a direct componentwise comparison of
\(\partial r\) and \(\partial r^{**}\) gives

$$
\tilde{\lambda}\in\partial r(\tilde{B})
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
|\tilde{B}_{ij}|\ge1
\quad\text{for all }i,j.
$$

Therefore, we obtain

$$
(\tilde{x},\tilde{B})\text{ is stationary for }(P)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
|\tilde{B}_{ij}|\ge1
\quad\forall\,i,j.
$$

Finally, recalling from \eqref{eq:pd-stationarity} that

$$
\tilde{\lambda}
=
\frac{\gamma}{n}\bigl(D(\tilde{x})-\tilde{B}\bigr),
$$

we obtain

$$
0\in
\frac{\gamma}{n}\bigl(\tilde{B}-D(\tilde{x})\bigr)
+\partial r^{**}(\tilde{B}).
$$

Thus, for every \(i,j\), \(\tilde{B}_{ij}\) is the unique minimizer of

$$
\min_{b\in\mathbb R}
\left\{
\frac{\gamma}{2n}
\bigl(b-D_{ij}(\tilde{x})\bigr)^2
+\mu(|b|-1)_+
\right\}.
$$

If \(|D_{ij}(\tilde{x})|<1\), the unique minimizer is
\(\tilde{B}_{ij}=D_{ij}(\tilde{x})\). If \(D_{ij}(\tilde{x})\ge1\), the minimizer cannot
lie below \(1\); similarly, if \(D_{ij}(\tilde{x})\le-1\), it cannot lie
above \(-1\). Hence, the following holds
$$
|\tilde{B}_{ij}|\ge1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
|D_{ij}(\tilde{x})|\ge1,
$$ which  combining the above equivalences yields
$$
(\tilde{x},\tilde{B})\text{ is stationary for }(P)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
r(\tilde{B})=r^{**}(\tilde{B})
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
|\tilde{B}_{ij}|\ge1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
|D_{ij}(\tilde{x})|\ge1, \text{ for all } i,j.
$$
\end{proof}
