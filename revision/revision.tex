\noindent Dear Editor-in-Chief,

We respectfully ask for reconsideration of the decision on our manuscript \#M187198, ``DualHash: A Stochastic Primal-Dual Algorithm with Theoretical Guarantee for Deep Hashing.'' While we sincerely appreciate the reviewers' and the Associate Editor's time and effort, we believe we can fully address all comments through minor or major revisions. 

Some of the concerns underlying the rejection decision may stem from a misunderstanding of the manuscript's formulation and theoretical results, partly due to our not presenting them clearly enough. Below, we clarify the reviewers' major concerns and present the revisions we made in response.


\section{Reviewer 2, Comment 1: Stationarity Relationship}

\noindent\textbullet\quad\textbf{Reviewer comment.} \emph{%
The key question is whether this transformed problem provides meaningful guarantees for the original nonconvex W-regularized hashing objective. I do not find this connection convincingly established. In particular, the paper should clarify whether there is  strong duality, zero duality gap, or at least a rigorous stationarity between the transformed primal--dual problem and the original objective.}

\vspace{0.5em}
\noindent\textbullet\quad\textbf{\red{Our response.} }
We thank the reviewer for this comment. 
Before proceeding, we recall the notation of the 
manuscript. Problem~(P) reads 
\[
\tag{P}
\min_{x,B}\ J(x,B)=F(x,B)+h(B),
\]
where $h(B)=\lambda\sum_{i,j}\bigl||B_{ij}|-1\bigr|$ is the nonconvex nonsmooth W-regularizer. 
The dual problem~(D) is obtained by introducing the splitting $Y=B$
and eliminating $Y$ via the Fenchel conjugate~$h^*$:
\[
\max_{\Lambda}\min_{x,B}\
\mathcal{L}(x,B,\Lambda),\qquad
\mathcal{L}(x,B,\Lambda)
:=F(x,B)+\langle B,\Lambda\rangle-h^*(\Lambda).
\tag{D}
\]
We also define the critical set
\[
\operatorname{crit}\mathcal{L}
:=\bigl\{(x,B,\Lambda):\
0\in\partial\mathcal{L}(x,B,\Lambda)\bigr\}.
% ,\quad\text{i.e.,}\quad
% \nabla_xF=0,\quad
% \nabla_BF+\Lambda=0,\quad
% B\in\partial h^*(\Lambda).
\]

In the submitted manuscript, Lemma~5.2 establishes a stationarity condition linking the transformed primal--dual problem and the original objective. Namely, when a zero-gap primal--dual optimal pair $(x^*,B^*,\Lambda^*)$ exists,
$(x^*,B^*,\Lambda^*)\in\operatorname{crit}\mathcal{L}$.  This motivates our complexity analysis for computing $\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{dist}(0,\,\partial\mathcal{L}(x^*,B^*,\Lambda^*))^2]\le\epsilon$, which is the main body of our theoretical analysis.

Indeed, we agree that discussing whether a zero-gap primal--dual optimal pair exists is important, as the zero duality gap can fail due to the nonconvex nature of the W-regularizer $h$ in Problem (P). We then illustrate this with a concrete instance.

\begin{example}\label{ex:gap}
Consider the one-dimensional instance
$p^* = \min_{b\in\mathbb{R}}\bigl\{\tfrac12 b^2+\bigl||b|-1\bigr|\bigr\}$,
i.e., $F(b)=\tfrac12 b^2$ and $\lambda=1$. The primal optimum is attained at
$b^*=\pm1$ with $p^*=\tfrac12$. Since
$\min_b\{\tfrac12 b^2+\Lambda b\}=-\tfrac12\Lambda^2$ and
$h^*(\Lambda)=|\Lambda|$ on $[-1,1]$,
\[
d^*=\sup_{|\Lambda|\le1}\Bigl\{-\tfrac12\Lambda^2-|\Lambda|\Bigr\}=0,
\]
attained at $\Lambda^*=0$. Hence $p^*-d^*=\tfrac12>0$: strong duality
between Problem~(P) and Problem~(D) fails, and no zero-gap primal--dual pair exists.
\end{example}

Nevertheless, the reviewer's question highlights an important
point: what does stationarity of the reformulated
problem~\textup{(D)} imply for the original
problem~\textup{(P)}? We appreciate this question, which
prompted us to establish the following explicit
characterization (proved in the Appendix).

\begin{proposition}[Stationarity transfer]\label{prop:transfer}
Let $(\tilde x,\widetilde B,\widetilde\Lambda)\in\operatorname{crit}\mathcal{L}$, i.e.,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:crit}
\nabla_xF(\tilde x,\widetilde B)=0,\qquad
\nabla_BF(\tilde x,\widetilde B)+\widetilde\Lambda=0,\qquad
\widetilde B\in\partial h^*(\widetilde\Lambda).
\end{equation}
Then the following statements are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item $(\tilde x,\widetilde B)$ is a stationary point of Problem~(P), i.e.,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pstat}
\nabla_xF(\tilde x,\widetilde B)=0,\qquad
0\in\nabla_BF(\tilde x,\widetilde B)+\partial h(\widetilde B);
\end{equation}
\item the Fenchel--Young equality in~Lemma (5.2) holds at
$(\widetilde B,\widetilde\Lambda)$:
$h(\widetilde B)+h^*(\widetilde\Lambda)=\langle\widetilde\Lambda,\widetilde B\rangle$;
\item $|\widetilde B_{ij}|\ge1$ for all $i,j$;
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

Here are some important points from the Proposition. 

(a) Under the conditions of Proposition~1, every critical point of~$\mathcal{L}$ is a first-order stationary point of Problem~(P). Targeting first-order stationarity is standard in nonconvex (nonsmooth) optimization, such as ADMM for nonconvex nonsmooth problems \cite{yang2017admm,zhu2024inexact,li2015global,hong2016convergence} and primal--dual methods with nonlinear operators~\cite{clason2019acceleration}.

(b) While the zero duality gap may fail, this is well recognized in nonconvex optimization: saddle points of the Lagrangian need not exist, as Example~\ref{ex:gap} concretely demonstrates. Moreover, our numerical experiments confirm that the primal--dual formulation is practically advantageous, yielding lower quantization errors and higher retrieval precision, which suggests that optimizing through the dual space is more effective than directly attacking the nonconvex W-regularizer in Problem (P). 

(c) The necessary and sufficient condition in Proposition~\ref{prop:transfer} is based on $h(B)=h^{**}(B)$, a fact from convex analysis. The convex-relaxation effect of conjugating a nonconvex penalty has been recognized~\cite{mollenhoff2015primal,guo2026preconditioned}. However, no one has explicitly characterized when this condition holds for a specific penalty, or whether it is also necessary, and provided a corresponding characterization.

\section*{Appendix: Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:transfer}}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 0: a consequence of \eqref{eq:crit}.}
Since $h^{**}$ is proper, closed and convex, and $(h^{**})^*=h^*$, the subgradient inversion rule~\cite[Theorem~23.5]{rockafellar1970convex} gives
\[
\widetilde{B}\in\partial h^*(\widetilde{\Lambda})
\iff
\widetilde{\Lambda}\in\partial h^{**}(\widetilde{B})
\iff
h^{**}(\widetilde{B})+h^*(\widetilde{\Lambda})=\langle\widetilde{\Lambda},\widetilde{B}\rangle .
\tag{A.1}
\]
In addition, \eqref{eq:crit} gives $\widetilde{\Lambda}=-\nabla_B F(\tilde{x},\widetilde{B})$, so \eqref{eq:pstat} holds if and only if $\widetilde{\Lambda}\in\partial h(\widetilde{B})$.

\emph{(b)$\iff$(c).} By (A.1), the Fenchel--Young equality for $h$ holds at $(\widetilde{B},\widetilde{\Lambda})$ if and only if $h(\widetilde{B})=h^{**}(\widetilde{B})$. Comparing $h$ with $h^{**}(B)=\lambda\sum_{i,j}(|B_{ij}|-1)_+$ componentwise, this is equivalent to $|\widetilde{B}_{ij}|\ge1$ for all $i,j$.

\emph{(b)$\Rightarrow$(a).} The equality $h(\widetilde{B})+h^*(\widetilde{\Lambda})=\langle\widetilde{\Lambda},\widetilde{B}\rangle$ together with the definition of $h^*$ means that $\widetilde{B}$ globally minimizes $h(\cdot)-\langle\widetilde{\Lambda},\cdot\rangle$. By Fermat's rule~\cite[Theorem~10.1]{rockafellar1998variational} and Proposition~A.2 of the manuscript, $\widetilde{\Lambda}\in\widehat{\partial}h(\widetilde{B})\subseteq\partial h(\widetilde{B})$, which is \eqref{eq:pstat}.

\emph{(a)$\Rightarrow$(c).} By Step~0, (a) means $\widetilde{\Lambda}\in\partial h(\widetilde{B})\cap\partial h^{**}(\widetilde{B})$. Both functions are separable, so it suffices to examine a scalar component $b=\widetilde{B}_{ij}$. If $|b|<1$, then $\partial h^{**}(b)=\{0\}$, whereas $\partial h(b)=\{-\lambda\,\mathrm{sgn}(b)\}$ for $b\neq0$ and $\partial h(0)=\{-\lambda,\lambda\}$; in all cases $0\notin\partial h(b)$, so the intersection is empty. Hence $|\widetilde{B}_{ij}|\ge1$ for all $i,j$.
\end{proof}
