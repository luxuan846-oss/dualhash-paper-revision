\paragraph{Optimization algorithms for deep hashing} Improving retrieval accuracy and reducing the discrepancy between continuous representations and binary hash codes have long been central
issues in deep hashing. One line of work adopts continuous relaxation
and introduces explicit quantization regularization on network outputs;
in particular, W-type regularizers such as $\||\mathbf{u}|-\mathbf{1}\|_1$
have been widely used to encourage binary-like activations
\cite{liuDeepSupervisedHashing,wang2023deep,zhuDeepHashingNetwork2016}.
Another line refines the discretization objective through greedy coding
schemes, central similarity targets, or proxy-based supervision
\cite{su2018greedy,yuanCentralSimilarityQuantization2020,MPDH2024,
semanticHashCenters2025}. Alternative strategies directly address binary
constraints by introducing auxiliary hash codes
\cite{liDeepSupervisedHashing,li2017deep,liFeatureLearningBased2016}.

Recent studies have further strengthened the backbone architecture to
improve feature quality, extending deep hashing from conventional CNNs
to hybrid CNN--Transformer frameworks \cite{he2024hybridhash},
Transformer-based hashing models
\cite{chen2021transhash,wu2025fth,liu2025capmh}, and state-space
architectures \cite{he2025mambahash}. Notably, even recent architectures
retain W-type regularization; for instance, \cite{he2025mambahash}
employs $\||\mathbf{u}|-\mathbf{1}\|_2^2$ as a smooth surrogate for
$\||\mathbf{u}|-\mathbf{1}\|_1$ to facilitate gradient-based
optimization, indicating that the practical relevance of W-type
regularization has not diminished. The remaining challenge is to
establish theoretically grounded optimization methods for directly
handling nonsmooth W-type regularization under nonlinear deep network
mappings, which is the focus of this work.

\paragraph{Primal-Dual Methods for Nonconvex structured problems} From a constrained optimization perspective, Problem \eqref{Eq: the general nonlinear composite problem in intro} can be reformulated using auxiliary variables, leading to a nonlinearly constrained problem. There has been growing interest in primal-dual methods for such problems, particularly augmented Lagrangian methods (ALM) and their stochastic variants. A Lagrangian-based framework was pioneered for deterministic settings by \cite{bolteNonconvexLagrangian2018}. This line of work was subsequently extended to stochastic settings in \cite{shi2025momentum, jin2022stochastic, lu2024variancereducedfirstordermethodsdeterministically}, which primarily address single-block problem structures. This type of block-separable structure is naturally amenable to the Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (ADMM), a technique that decomposes complex problems into simpler subproblems. Originally developed for convex optimization \cite{gabay1976dual}, ADMM has well-established convergence guarantees and complexity analyses in the convex setting \cite{he20121,monteiro2013iteration}. Driven by its empirical success in nonconvex applications such as neural network training \cite{taylor2016training}, nonconvex ADMM and its associated complexity analysis have attracted significant research attention \cite{li2015global,hong2016convergence,Boct2019SIOPT,boct2020proximal}. Notably, \cite{huang2019faster} provided a pioneering analysis that established gradient (or sample) complexity bounds for nonconvex stochastic ADMM.

\paragraph{Complexity results for nonconvex stochastic constrained optimization}
% \textit{Compositional structure with nonlinear mappings:} The key technical hurdle in handling \eqref{Eq: the general nonlinear composite problem in intro} is ensuring tractability of the composite function \(h(\bD_i)\). Existing stochastic methods for nonlinear compositions \cite{duchiStochasticMethodsComposite2018,hu2023non,wang2024complexity,ADMMNolinear2024} typically require one of the following:
% \textbf{(C1)} \textit{Weak convexity of the composition}: \(h(\bD_i)\) is \(\rho\)-weakly convex, enabling smoothing via Moreau envelopes;
% \textbf{(C2)} \textit{Convexity of the outer function}: \(h\) is convex (even if nonsmooth), allowing efficient proximal operators.
% Other studies \cite{bian2021stochastic,Huang2020FasterSA} focus on stochastic optimization with linear compositional structures.
% In deep hashing, neither condition holds: W-type regularizers like \(\|\,|\bz| - \mathbf{1}\|_1\) are \textit{nonconvex} (violating C2), and their composition with neural network outputs \(\bD_i(\bx)\) is \textit{not weakly convex} (violating C1) due to the non-Lipschitz gradient of \(|\cdot|\) around zero. This regime—nonconvex outer function \(h\) with nonlinear inner mapping \(\bD_i\)—remains largely unaddressed in the stochastic optimization literature. Bolte et al. \cite{bolteNonconvexLagrangian2018} study a Lagrangian-based approach for \eqref{Eq: the general nonlinear composite problem in intro} in the deterministic setting, establishing asymptotic convergence via the Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz property. However, their analysis neither extends to stochastic gradients nor provides non-asymptotic complexity bounds—both critical for assessing algorithmic efficiency in modern deep learning applications.
