\section{Reviewer's general assessment}

\noindent\textit{Summary.}
{\itshape The paper presents DualHash, a primal-dual reformulation for deep hashing with W-type quantization regularization. My understanding is that the main contribution is an adaptation of primal-dual splitting, combined with stochastic momentum/STORM updates, to deep hashing. The method uses the Fenchel conjugate of a specific W-type regularizer to obtain a closed-form dual proximal update. However, the convexity of the dual/conjugate side is standard.}
\par\smallskip
\normalfont\upshape\responseLabel We thank the reviewer for this assessment, which allows us to highlight our contributions better. While we agree that the convexity of the conjugate is standard, the \rv{submitted manuscript} did not clearly convey what is actually novel, namely the relationship between the primal--dual reformulation and the original nonconvex W-regularized hashing objective. The revised version now focuses on this relationship, and its core contributions are as follows.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{(\arabic*)},leftmargin=2.2em,before=\let\item\reviewitem]
\item \textbf{We define the W-type class covering the W-type regularizers widely used in deep hashing, unifying their structural analysis.} While deep hashing methods use various W-type regularizers, our analysis depends only on their common shape, which \green{Definition~\mref{def:wtype}} formalizes by three conditions (\green{Table~\mref{tab:wtype}}). \green{Lemma~\mref{lem:wtype-conj}} gives the convex envelope and the conjugate for the whole class, which support the results in \green{Section~\mref{Section: Methodology}}.
\item \textbf{We establish the stationarity relations between the primal--dual reformulation and the original hashing objective, clarifying what meaningful guarantees the reformulation provides.} While the conjugate is convex, $h$ is not, and the duality gap can be positive (\green{Example~\mref{ex:gap}}), so standard duality arguments do not directly apply. \green{Section~\mref{Section: Methodology}} establishes these relations without zero duality gap, as detailed in \hyperref[sec:r1-relation]{our response in Section~\ref*{sec:r1-relation}} below.
\item \textbf{We show that the quantization error, a hashing-specific quantity, controls a structural error that does not vanish with the KKT residual, providing theoretical support for reducing the quantization error in practice.} The quantization error~\green{\meq{eq:quantization-error}} is widely reported in deep hashing but is often treated as an empirical measure. \green{Theorem~\mref{prop:original-certificate}} shows that, for the regularizers in Table~\mref{tab:wtype}, it bounds the structural error, the part of the stationarity error for the original problem that reducing the KKT residual alone may not remove. Since $Q_1(\bx)$ measures the distance between the network outputs and their binary codes $\operatorname{sgn}(\calD(\bx))$, this result relates the quality of the final binary codes to the stationarity guarantee for the original problem. Designing W-type regularizers or output layers that further reduce this error is an interesting future direction, for which we believe \green{Theorem~\mref{prop:original-certificate}} provides a natural criterion.
\item \textbf{Based on the min--max problem, we establish oracle complexity guarantees, which, to our knowledge, have been lacking for widely used W-type regularized deep hashing methods.} While DualHash builds on primal--dual splitting and the momentum and STORM estimators, existing complexity results cover simpler settings, such as tractable proximal mappings or weakly convex compositions, which $h\circ\calD$ need not satisfy. Our analysis handles this stochastic, nonconvex, nonsmooth, multi-block setting through the single-valued proximal mapping of $h^*$ and Lyapunov functions that control the possible increase of $\calL$ under the dual update.
\end{enumerate}

\normalfont\upshape\responseLabel

\begin{bluequote}
\textbf{Definition~\mref{def:wtype} (W-type regularizer).} Let $\phi:\RR\to[0,+\infty)$. \begin{enumerate}[label=(W\arabic*),leftmargin=3em,before=\let\item\reviewitem]
\item $\phi$ is even and continuous, and $\phi(t)=0$ if and only if $t\in\{-1,1\}$;
\item $\phi$ is convex on $[1,+\infty)$;
\item $\phi$ decreases at a nonvanishing rate on $[0,1)$: for every $\theta\in(0,1)$ there exists $c_{\theta}>0$ such that $\phi(s)-\phi(t)\ge c_{\theta}(t-s)$ for all $0\le s<t\le\theta$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{bluequote}

\section{Relation between the primal--dual reformulation and the original objective}\label{sec:r1-relation}

\begin{itemize}\itshape
\item The key question is whether this transformed problem provides meaningful guarantees for the original nonconvex W-regularized hashing objective. I do not find this connection convincingly established. In particular, the paper should clarify whether there is strong duality, zero duality gap, or at least a rigorous stationarity relationship between the transformed primal-dual problem and the original objective.
\par\smallskip
\normalfont\upshape\responseLabel
{
We sincerely appreciate this key question, which led to the central results of the revision. The submitted manuscript (Lemma~5.2) already showed that, if the gap is zero, the optimal pair is a KKT point of $\calL$, but it did not develop this relation further. The revision states this result clearly as \green{Proposition~\mref{prop:zerogap}} and, since the gap can be positive for W-type regularizers, further establishes relations that do not rely on it. For the reviewer's convenience, we list the four problems involved:
\begin{align*}
&\min_{\bx}\ J_0(\bx):=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n f_i(\bx)+h(\calD(\bx)),\tag{P0}\\
&\min_{\bx,\bB}\ J(\bx,\bB):=F(\bx,\bB)+h(\bB),\tag{P}\\
&\min_{\bx,\bB}\max_{\bLmbd}\ \calL(\bx,\bB,\bLmbd):=F(\bx,\bB)+\langle\bB,\bLmbd\rangle-h^*(\bLmbd),\tag{\mref{Eq: minmax problem}}\\
&\max_{\bLmbd}\min_{\bx,\bB}\ \calL(\bx,\bB,\bLmbd).\tag{D}
\end{align*}
The dual of Problem~(P) is Problem~(D); our algorithms target KKT points of the associated min--max problem~\green{\meq{Eq: minmax problem}}. Based on this problem, we establish in \green{Sections~\mref{Sec: original optimality relations} and~\mref{Sec: original objective guarantees}}, for the whole W-type class, the relations from the min--max problem to Problem~(P) and then to Problem~(P0). We now answer the reviewer's questions in turn.

\textbf{Zero duality gap and strong duality are equivalent here, but the gap can be positive for W-type regularizers.} Both the primal and the dual problems admit optimal solutions, so the two notions coincide. As noted above, a zero gap yields a KKT point of $\calL$. For W-type regularizers, however, the gap can be positive even with a convex $F$, as the following example shows \green{(Example~\mref{ex:gap}, p.~\mpage{ex:gap})}:
\begin{bluequote}
\textbf{Example~\mref{ex:gap}.} Consider the problem
\[
p^*=\min_{b\in\RR}\Bigl\{\tfrac12 b^2+\bigl||b|-1\bigr|\Bigr\}.
\]
Its optimal value is $p^*=\tfrac12$, attained at $b^*=\pm1$. The dual value is
\[
d^*=\sup_{|\Lambda|\le1}\Bigl\{-\tfrac12\Lambda^2-|\Lambda|\Bigr\}=0,
\]
attained at $\Lambda^*=0$. This follows from $\min_b\{\tfrac12 b^2+\Lambda b\}=-\tfrac12\Lambda^2$ and $h^*(\Lambda)=|\Lambda|$ on $[-1,1]$ ($+\infty$ elsewhere). Hence $p^*-d^*=\tfrac12>0$, and no zero-gap primal--dual pair exists.
\end{bluequote}
The gap arises because $h^{**}(b)=(|b|-1)_+$ vanishes on $[-1,1]$, so $(b,\Lambda)=(0,0)$ is a KKT point although $b=0$ is not stationary for the original problem. This motivates a stationarity relationship that does not rely on zero duality gap.

\textbf{In response to this question, we establish the requested stationarity relationship at two levels.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em,before=\let\item\reviewitem]
\item \textbf{At the exact level, a KKT point of $\calL$ is stationary for Problem~(P) if and only if $|\tilde b_{ij}|\ge1$ for all $i,j$.} We give this criterion in \green{Proposition~\mref{prop:transfer}}, which does not require zero duality gap \green{(p.~\mpage{prop:transfer})}:
\begin{bluequote}
\textbf{Proposition~\mref{prop:transfer}.} Let $h$ be a W-type regularizer, and let $(\tilde{\bx},\tilde{\bB},\tilde{\bLmbd})$ be a KKT point of $\calL$. The following statements are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)},leftmargin=2em,before=\let\item\reviewitem]
\item $(\tilde{\bx},\tilde{\bB})$ is a stationary point of Problem~(P), i.e., $\nabla_{\bx}F(\tilde{\bx},\tilde{\bB})=\zero$ and $\zero\in\nabla_{\bB}F(\tilde{\bx},\tilde{\bB})+\partial h(\tilde{\bB})$;
\item $h(\tilde{\bB})+h^*(\tilde{\bLmbd})=\langle\tilde{\bLmbd},\tilde{\bB}\rangle$;
\item $|\tilde b_{ij}|\ge1$ for all $i,j$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{bluequote}

\item \textbf{At the approximate level, an approximate KKT point is near-stationary for Problem~(P0), up to an error controlled by the quantization error.} Our algorithms aim to find an approximate KKT point $(\bx,\bB,\bLmbd)$ of $\calL$, whose accuracy is measured by the KKT residual $r_{\calK}:=\dist(\zero,\calK(\bx,\bB,\bLmbd))$.
