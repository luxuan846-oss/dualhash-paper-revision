\noindent Dear Professor Chouzenoux,

\medskip
Thank you for your willingness to handle our substantially revised manuscript, now entitled ``DualHash: Stochastic Primal-Dual Algorithms for W-Type Regularized Deep Hashing.'' We appreciate your consideration and the reviewers' careful evaluation. Their comments have prompted substantial revisions to the theoretical analysis and presentation.

The central concern was whether the primal--dual formulation provides meaningful guarantees for the original nonconvex hashing objective. We agree that this connection was insufficiently established in the submitted manuscript. The revision addresses this concern and revises the stochastic analysis. The principal changes are summarized below, followed by our point-by-point responses.

\section*{Summary of the Major Revisions}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,before=\let\item\reviewitem]
\item \textbf{Definition and scope of W-type regularization.} Section~\mref{Section: Problem Reformulation} defines the class through structural conditions and presents representative regularizers with their conjugates and proximal mappings. We distinguish the structural results for this class from the additional assumptions used in the complexity analysis and the particular regularizer used in the experiments.
\item \textbf{Guarantees for the original hashing objective.} Section~\mref{Section: Methodology} distinguishes the original objective, its quadratic-penalty relaxation, and the min--max formulation. We characterize when KKT stationarity transfers to the penalty model and establish a near-stationarity guarantee for the original objective, separating optimization error from structural error controlled by quantization error. Section~\mref{Sec: guarantees original objective} combines this guarantee with the oracle complexity bounds and states the quantization-error condition needed for small near-stationarity tolerances.
\item \textbf{Revised stochastic analysis.} Section~\mref{Section: Convergence analysis} and Appendices~\mref{app:complexity-auxiliary-results}--\mref{app:storm-complexity} specify the sampling and expectation framework, revise the gradient-error estimates, and provide lower bounds for the potentials used in the analysis. The initialization, parameter conditions, and treatment of the finite dataset size are also made explicit.
\item \textbf{Organization and interpretation.} We consolidate the model definitions and preliminaries in Section~\mref{Section: Problem Reformulation}, place the optimality relationships before the algorithms, and move several intermediate estimates into the appendices. Section~\mref{Section: Numerical experiments} clarifies the quantization-error metric and distinguishes the practical dual-step-size variant from the scheme covered by the analysis.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Reviewer's general assessment}

\noindent\textit{Summary.}
 {The paper presents DualHash, a primal-dual reformulation for deep hashing with W-type quantization regularization. My understanding is that the main contribution is an adaptation of primal-dual splitting, combined with stochastic momentum/STORM updates, to deep hashing. The method uses the Fenchel conjugate of a specific W-type regularizer to obtain a closed-form dual proximal update. However, the convexity of the dual/conjugate side is standard.}
\par\smallskip
\normalfont\upshape\responseLabel We thank the reviewer for this assessment. We agree that convexity of a Fenchel conjugate is a standard fact and is not, by itself, our contribution. The revision focuses on what this dual treatment preserves and what it loses for nonconvex W-type regularization. Section~\mref{Section: Methodology} establishes the exact stationarity relationship and the original-objective near-stationarity bound; Section~\mref{Sec: guarantees original objective} connects that bound to the algorithmic output. The stochastic analysis controls the coupled primal, dual, and estimator errors. We explain these points in the response below.

\section{Major issues}

\subsubsection*{(a) Relation between the primal-dual reformulation and the original objective}

\begin{itemize}\itshape
\item The key question is whether this transformed problem provides meaningful guarantees for the original nonconvex W-regularized hashing objective. I do not find this connection convincingly established. In particular, the paper should clarify whether there is strong duality, zero duality gap, or at least a rigorous stationarity relationship between the transformed primal-dual problem and the original objective.
\par\smallskip
\normalfont\upshape\responseLabel
{
We thank the reviewer for this key question. We agree that the submitted manuscript did not adequately establish the connection to the original hashing objective. The revision distinguishes the original objective (P0), the quadratic-penalty model (P), and the min--max formulation. Their relationships are established in Sections~\mref{Sec: original optimality relations} and~\mref{Sec: original objective guarantees}.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{(\roman*)},leftmargin=2.2em]
\item \textbf{Duality gap.} Proposition~\mref{prop:zerogap} shows that a primal--dual optimal pair with zero duality gap is a KKT point of $\calL$. The duality gap decomposes as
\[
p^*-d^*=(p^*-p^{**})+(p^{**}-d^*),
\]
where $p^{**}$ is the optimal value after replacing $h$ by $h^{**}$. Example~\mref{ex:gap} gives a scalar instance with convex $F$ and a positive gap of $1/2$. Thus the formulation does not provide an unconditional strong-duality guarantee.
\item \textbf{Exact stationarity for the penalty model.} Proposition~\mref{prop:transfer} characterizes when a KKT point of $\calL$ is stationary for (P), without requiring zero duality gap. This result holds for the W-type class defined in Definition~\mref{def:wtype}. The proof in Appendix~\mref{app:transfer-proof} uses the conjugate characterization and condition (W3), through Lemma~\mref{lem:no-inner-stationary}; it is not restricted to the $\ell_1$ formula.
\item \textbf{Approximate stationarity for the original objective.} Theorem~\mref{prop:original-certificate} gives a nearby-point certificate for $J_0$. For $L\ge L_f+\|\bLmbd\|L_{\nabla\calD}$, $L>0$, and $\kappa=(1+m^2)/(4m)$, it gives a point $\widehat{\bx}$ satisfying
\[
\|\widehat{\bx}-\bx\|\le\sqrt{\frac2L}\left(\sqrt\kappa\,r_{\calK}+\sqrt{h(\calD(\bx))}\right),
\]
\[
\dist(\zero,\partial J_0(\widehat{\bx}))\le
\left(\sqrt{1+G_{\calD}^2}+2\sqrt{2\kappa L}\right)r_{\calK}
+2\sqrt{2Lh(\calD(\bx))}.
\]
The first term is controlled by the KKT residual; the second need not vanish with that residual. For the $\ell_1$ W-regularizer, $h(\calD(\bx))=\lambda Q_1(\bx)$. With a compact dual domain, one can choose $L=L_0:=L_f+\lambda\sqrt{nd}L_{\nabla\calD}$; the structural-error term then has no explicit dependence on $\gamma$. Corollary~\mref{cor:original-stationarity} combines this certificate with the oracle bounds. In particular, $\lambda\EE Q_1(\bx^R)=\calO(\varepsilon^2)$ is a sufficient additional condition for both expected near-stationarity tolerances to be $\calO(\varepsilon)$; the KKT complexity bound alone does not ensure this quantization-error condition.
\end{enumerate}
For completeness, the exact stationarity statement is reproduced below \green{(Section~\mref{Sec: original optimality relations}, p.~\mpage{prop:transfer}; proof in Appendix~\mref{app:transfer-proof}, p.~\mpage{app:transfer-proof})}.
\begin{bluequote}
\textbf{Proposition \mref{prop:transfer}.} Let $h$ be a W-type regularizer, and let $(\tilde{\bx},\tilde{\bB},\tilde{\bLmbd})$ be a KKT point of $\calL$. The following statements are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
\item $(\tilde{\bx},\tilde{\bB})$ is a stationary point of Problem~(P), i.e., $\nabla_{\bx}F(\tilde{\bx},\tilde{\bB})=\bm0$ and $\bm0\in\nabla_{\bB}F(\tilde{\bx},\tilde{\bB})+\partial h(\tilde{\bB})$;
\item $h(\tilde{\bB})+h^*(\tilde{\bLmbd})=\langle\tilde{\bLmbd},\tilde{\bB}\rangle$;
\item $|\tilde b_{ij}|\ge1$ for all $i,j$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{bluequote}
}
\end{itemize}

\item {\itshape (page 13) Give more details to justify

\normalfont\upshape\responseLabel
We thank the reviewer for this comment. The bound (5.11) (now \meq{eq: the gradient error in SGDM}) follows from the standard variance formula for uniform sampling. With the revised probabilistic framework and conditional-expectation notation above, its derivation reads as follows.
\begin{quote}\normalfont\upshape
\textit{Proof.} Recall that the stochastic gradient error $\be^k$ of Algorithm~\mref{alg:our_algorithm_sgdm} is defined as
\[
\be^k
=
\frac{1}{b_k}\sum_{i\in\calJ_k}
\left(
\nabla_{\bx}F_i(\bz^k,\bb_i^k)
-
\nabla_{\bx}F(\bz^k,\bB^k)
\right).
\]
Since $(\bz^k,\bB^k)$ is $\calF_k$-measurable and
$\calJ_k$ is sampled uniformly without replacement and independently
of $\calF_k$, define
\[
\bv_i^k
:=
\nabla_{\bx}F_i(\bz^k,\bb_i^k)
-
\nabla_{\bx}F(\bz^k,\bB^k).
\]
Conditionally on $\calF_k$, Assumption~\mref{Ass: main assumptions}
gives
\[
\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \bv_i^k=\zero,
\qquad
\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n\|\bv_i^k\|^2\leq\sigma^2
\quad\text{a.s.}
\]
For $n\geq2$, the variance formula for uniform sampling without replacement
therefore yields
\[
\EE_k[\be^k]=\zero,
\]
and
\[
\EE_k\!\left[\|\be^k\|^2\right]
=
